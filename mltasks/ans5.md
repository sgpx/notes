# Vectors: notation, geometric interpretation, addition, scalar multiplication

**Question:** Let [[\mathbf{u} = (2, -1)]] and [[\mathbf{v} = (-4, 2)]]. 

1. Write [[\mathbf{u}]] and [[\mathbf{v}]] in unit-vector notation using [[\mathbf{i}]] and [[\mathbf{j}]].
2. Compute [[\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}]] and [[3\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}]].
3. Determine the geometric interpretation of [[\mathbf{v}]] relative to [[\mathbf{u}]]: are they parallel, antiparallel, or neither? Explain your reasoning.
4. For the vector [[\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}]], state its magnitude and whether it points in the same direction as [[\mathbf{u}]], the opposite direction, or neither.

Show all steps clearly.

**Answer:**

# solution

## part 1

u = 2i-j, v=-4i+2j

## part 2

P1 = u+v = -2i+j
P2=3u-v = 3*(2i-j) + 4i-2j = 6i-3j+4i-2j = 10i-5j

## part 3, geometric

u.v = |u||v|cos(theta)

=> theta = arccos(u.v/|u||v|)

u.v = -8 - 2 = -10
|u| = sqrt(4+1) = sqrt(5)
|v| = sqrt(16+4) = sqrt(20) = 2*sqrt(5)

theta = arccos(-10/2*sqrt(5)*sqrt(5) = arccos(-1)
theta = 180 degrees

therefore u and v are antiparallel

## part 4

let q = u+v

q = -2i + j

u = 2i - j = -q

by observation we know that vector A and -A are at an angle of 180 deg, therefore u and q are pointing in the opposite directions

since u = -q

|u| = |-q| = |-1*q| = |-1|*|q| = |q|

=> |q| = sqrt(5)


therefore q=-u has a magnitude equal to |u| = sqrt(5), but points in the opposite direction
